About Jar of Marbles

Small acts lead to something bigger.

The idea behind it is borrowed from Brené Brown's "marble jar theory" I came across a few years ago. The theory says "...trust is earned slowly through small, consistent actions rather than large, dramatic gestures."

Jar of Marbles takes that idea and turns it into a shared practice. It gives your circle one jar to fill together, a calm place to notice and share the moments that strengthen your relationships and remind you of the kindness already present in your lives.

The App

Marbles: Any time someone in your circle shows up, listens, keeps a promise, respects your privacy, or is kind, you drop a marble into the shared jar with a message about the moment and what it meant to you. The messages stay sealed.

The jar: One jar, filled by the whole circle. Every marble anyone adds goes into the same jar, and nobody reads the messages until it's full.

Pulling one out: Trust can be hurt too. If someone in the circle hurt you, you can pull a marble out, which brings the count down. You'll be asked to say why. Nobody sees who pulled it, you'll never see the message that was attached to it, and your reason is shared with everyone when the jar is opened.

Opening the jar: When the jar is full, the circle decides together when to meet, then opens it together. That's when the messages are finally read, and the reasons for any marbles pulled out are shared too. Order a pizza, sit down, and share one marble at a time.

Circle: This is your circle of trust. It belongs to the people in it, and the person who starts it manages the group. Only people in the circle can fill its jar.

The aim of the app isn't the full jar itself. Our aim is to make us pause, reflect, and recognize the kindness, love, and trust we share every day in small doses, instead of waiting for one big gesture.

Why I created it

I believe love and trust don't arrive all at once. Our attention naturally moves toward what is urgent, difficult, or missing. Jar of Marbles creates a small counterweight: a place to deliberately hold onto what was generous, thoughtful, loving, or simply good even when it isn't immediately visible.

I'm an independent dreamer with a goal to give back more than I've taken from the world. Jar of Marbles is one small way I'm trying to do that: a place to notice and hold onto the kindness we so often overlook.
