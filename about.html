About Jar of Marbles

Small acts become something bigger.

The metaphor is borrowed from Brené Brown's "marble jar theory" I came across a few years ago. It explains how "...trust is earned slowly through small, consistent actions rather than large, dramatic gestures."

Jar of Marbles turns that metaphor into a personal practice. It gives you a calm place to notice and remember the moments that strengthen your relationships and remind you of the kindness already present in your life.

The idea

Adding Marbles: Every time someone in your circle shows up for you, listens, keeps a promise, respects your privacy, or is kind, add a marble to their jar to let them know what that moment meant to you.

Removing Marbles: On days when your own capacity for kindness is running low, borrow a marble. Let it remind you of the kindness you've given and received, and still have around you.

The aim is not to reach a full jar but to work on building trust by showing up for people in your circle no matter the size of the moment, big or small.

Once your jar is full, it's time to celebrate with your circle! Order a pizza, sit down, and share one marble at a time.

Why I created it

Our attention naturally moves toward what is urgent, difficult, or missing. Jar of Marbles creates a small counterweight: a place to deliberately hold onto what was generous, thoughtful, loving, or simply good.

Jar of Marbles is an independent project that is part of my personal mission, and is not affiliated with or endorsed by Brené Brown or anyone else.
